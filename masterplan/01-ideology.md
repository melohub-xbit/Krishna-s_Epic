# 01 · Ideology — one book, two civilisations

## The thesis

The portfolio is a **manga volume about a Telugu engineer**. Japan supplies the
*form* — the book, the panel grammar, the ink, the page-turn. Telugu/Hindu
culture supplies the *content and soul* — the deities, the chakra, the kolam,
the epics that structure the work. Neither culture decorates the other; each
owns a layer:

| Layer | Owner | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Format & grammar** | Japan | volume, spreads, koma, gutters, RTL flips, screentone, SFX lettering, omake |
| **Ink & craft** | Japan | sumi-e strokes, washi grain, hanko stamps, wagara patterns, ensō |
| **Iconography & story** | Telugu/Hindu | chakra, kolam, torana, conch, bow, Ramayanam/Mahabharatam spine |
| **Colour & warmth** | Telugu | gold, saffron, kumkum, turmeric — the temple palette IS the palette |

This division is why the fusion works instead of clashing: a manga printed in
temple colours, telling a Telugu story. Alche does the same split — Japanese
soul, international web grammar.

## Why these two cultures actually rhyme (use these rhymes; don't invent others)

Researched convergences — each one is a legitimate design bridge:

1. **Vermilion.** Torii gates are painted vermilion to ward off evil; kumkum
   and sindoor are vermilion for the same protective role. One red, two
   traditions → the kumkum-red accent reads as both.
2. **The gate as threshold.** Torii marks passage into sacred space; the torana
   garland marks a blessed doorway. Both say "you are crossing into somewhere
   else" → every major navigation is a gate-crossing.
3. **The seal.** Japanese hanko: a red name-stamp that authenticates a work.
   Indian mudra/seal tradition + the existing కృ seal. A **Telugu hanko** is
   the site's signature mark — fully authentic to both.
4. **Sacred geometry.** Wagara (asanoha, seigaiha, kikkō) and kolam/rangoli are
   both meditative, rule-based, repeated geometry drawn as blessing. They can
   sit side by side in one background lattice.
5. **Impermanence.** Sakura's mono no aware (the samurai's transient life) and
   the kolam's daily erasure at dawn — both cultures ritualise beauty that
   doesn't last. This is the emotional register of transitions: things bloom,
   scatter, and are redrawn.
6. **The circle.** Ensō (the one-stroke Zen circle) and the chakra (the wheel
   of time). The ensō can be drawn *around* the chakra without either losing
   meaning.
7. **Warrior discipline.** Bushidō's eight virtues and the kshatriya dharma of
   the epics — Rama's bow and the samurai's blade carry the same ethic.
   Discipline is the *About* section's frame.

## Principles (inherited + extended)

1. **One stage, many states** — content transitions in place; the reader turns
   pages, the book never becomes a scrolling document.
2. **Flow over pages** — every hop is a named, choreographed move.
3. **Everything is crafted** — see [[detail-standard]]; authentic form first,
   ornate execution always.
4. **Alive & reactive** — the world breathes; the seal is the cursor.
5. **Two voices, one world** — professional = disciplined seinen ink; personal
   = omake warmth (poster + cel).
6. **NEW — Ma (間).** Restraint is a feature. Between big moves, calm holds.
   Gutters, silence, and empty paper are content. Alche's power is how *little*
   moves between its big moments.
7. **NEW — Every symbol earns its place.** A Japanese element enters only via a
   rhyme in the table above. No katana-for-katana's-sake; no pagoda clip-art.

## What this is not

- Not a Japan-themed site with Indian bits, nor the reverse. The split of
  layers above is the arbiter of every "where does X go" argument.
- Not a museum. It's a shōnen/seinen volume about real work — projects are
  chapters with stakes, villains (the problem), and astras (the tech).
